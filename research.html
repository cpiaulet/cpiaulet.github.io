I study exoplanets
and their atmospheres.

I am both an observer and a theorist. I use space- and ground-based telescopes
together with interior and atmosphere models to learn what small planets are made of,
whether they keep atmospheres, and how their compositions trace back to where and how they formed.

Water worlds &
sub-Neptunes

Many planets between the size of Earth and Neptune are too low-density to be pure rock. I combine JWST spectroscopy with coupled interior–atmosphere models to measure how much water and other volatiles these planets hold, and where that water came from. This is the focus of my 51 Pegasi b Fellowship.

  • ApJ 2026 A window for water–hydrogen demixing on warm, metal-rich sub-Neptunes · press release
  • ApJL 2024 A water-rich “steam world” atmosphere on GJ 9827 d · press release
  • Nature Astronomy 2023 Evidence for a volatile-rich 1.5-Earth-radius planet (Kepler-138 d) · press release

Rocky worlds &
habitability

Which rocky planets can hold on to or revive atmospheres? What are the ingredients required for planetary habitability, and the standards to claim biosignature detections? Below are a few studies I led and contributed to that address these fundamental questions.

Stars in
the way

Starspots and faculae on the host star leave imprints that can mimic or hide a planet’s atmosphere. I build computational tools to separate stellar and planetary signals, essential when studying planets around active stars.

  • JOSS, in review STCTM: a framework for stellar contamination and stellar spectra · website
  • ApJL 2025 Promise and peril: stellar contamination on TRAPPIST-1 c
  • ApJL 2023 Strong stellar contamination in TRAPPIST-1 b spectra

WASP-107 b. Illustration: NASA, ESA, CSA, R. Crawford (STScI)

Giant planets,
formation & escape

Low-density giants and super-puffs are natural laboratories for envelope accretion, migration, and atmospheric escape. I lead a panchromatic JWST survey of super-puffs and study escaping helium and atmospheric chemistry on keystone gas giants.

  • Nature Astronomy 2026 Continuous helium absorption from the tails of WASP-107b · press release
  • AJ 2021 WASP-107b’s density is even lower · press release
  • Nature 2023 First detection of CO2 in an exoplanet atmosphere (JWST ERS)
